Historical Background

The geopolitical and industrial backdrop leading up to the crisis at Mead Nuclear Plant.

Era: 1960s

The 10 Nuclear Plants Initiative

In the 1960s, the Department of Energy and Coal Industry decided to construct at least 10 nuclear power plants across the republic to accommodate rapidly increasing energy consumption and population growth. Mead Nuclear Plant was commissioned as a cornerstone of this ambitious national grid.

Infrastructure

Lake Mead & Koshulyn Dam

The plant was established near Lake Mead, an artificially created water body formed by the massive Koshulyn Dam, named in honor of President Yaroslav Koshulyn. The dam provides essential cooling water flow and auxiliary hydroelectric generation for the complex.

Conflict: 1996

The 1996 Frontline Crisis

In 1996, the Mexican Army invaded the southern states of New Sloboda under the pretext of «liberating» the local Mexican minority—despite the population being overwhelmingly English and Ukrainian. Positioned perilously close to the frontline, Mead Nuclear Plant now faces direct assault as enemies aim to blow up the facility and blackout the capital.
